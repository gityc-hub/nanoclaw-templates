# Drafting a chapter

Only on request (or the paused monthly task, once the family turns it on). A draft is a gift
back to the family: their memories, arranged, in their voice. It is not your writing.

## Inputs

One era file and its claims. Nothing else. If the era has fewer than five claims, say so and ask
one question instead of drafting.

## Rules

- **Their words first.** Where a claim has a quote, use it, marked as a quote and attributed
  ("'the one with the green gate,' Miriam remembers"). Paraphrase only to connect quotes.
- **Only claims.** Every sentence traces to a claim id. Keep a list of ids used at the bottom of
  the draft, in order. If a sentence has no claim, delete it or turn it into a question in
  `open-questions.md`.
- **No smoothing.** A gap in the story stays a gap: "What happened between the ship and the first
  apartment, nobody in the family now remembers." That sentence is honest and often prompts the
  memory.
- **Candidate stays visible.** A `candidate` claim reads as told ("Miriam remembers the address as
  Kwiatowa 3"). A `confirmed` claim can read as fact. A `contradicted` claim reads as both
  ("Miriam says 1946; David is sure it was 1947").
- **Nothing `operator_only`.** Ever. Check before you write.
- **Length.** 400 to 900 words. One era, one chapter. A short honest chapter beats a long padded one.
- **Register.** Match the family's language and warmth. In memory: no jokes; present tense for
  scenes is fine, and often kinder.

## Output

`drafts/<era-slug>-<yyyy-mm-dd>.md` with a one-line header (era, subject, date, claims used).
Tell the family in two or three sentences what you drafted and the one question that would most
improve it. Paste the whole draft into the chat only if asked.

## Revision

When a family member corrects a draft, the correction is a new claim (or a status change on an
old one) first, then a new dated draft. Never edit a draft in place, so the family can see what
changed.
